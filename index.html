I am a Ph.D. candidate at the University of Alabama at Birmingham, advised by Prof. Yanni Zeng. My research is in nonlinear partial differential equations, in particular global existence and asymptotic stability for chemotaxis models arising in mathematical biology. I also work on problems in computational mathematics, data science, and scientific machine learning.

I have taught mathematics for over ten years, at UAB, Florida Atlantic University, and Tribhuvan University in Nepal, including Intermediate Algebra, Precalculus, Calculus, and Probability and Statistics. I expect to complete my Ph.D. in Spring 2027 and am currently seeking tenure-track faculty positions.

Research

Dissertation

Global Existence of Solutions on the Quarter Plane for a Chemotaxis Model with Logistic Growth and Logarithmic Sensitivity.

I study a Keller–Segel type system with logarithmic sensitivity and logistic growth on the half-line \(x>0\), of the form

$$ \begin{cases} u_t = D\,u_{xx} - \chi\,\big(u\,(\ln v)_x\big)_x + a\,u\left(1-\dfrac{u}{K}\right),\\[6pt] v_t = \varepsilon\, v_{xx} - \mu\, u\, v, \end{cases} \qquad x>0,\ t>0, $$

subject to initial and boundary data. The aim is to establish global existence of solutions and their asymptotic stability. The analysis combines pointwise Green's function estimates in the Liu–Zeng / Liu–Yu framework with a Nakamura–Nishibata boundary-cancellation energy method, extended to the fully nonlinear setting.
