About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Mathematics at the University of Alabama at Birmingham, advised by Prof. Yanni Zeng. My research is in nonlinear partial differential equations, in particular global existence of solutions for chemotaxis models arising in mathematical biology. I also work on problems in computational mathematics, data science, and scientific machine learning.

I have taught mathematics for over ten years, at UAB, Florida Atlantic University, and Himalaya College of Engineering (affiliated to Tribhuvan University) in Nepal, including Intermediate Algebra, Precalculus, Calculus, and Probability and Statistics. I expect to complete my Ph.D. in Spring 2027 and am currently seeking tenure-track faculty positions.

Teaching

I want students to understand why mathematics works, not only how to carry out procedures. Whenever I can, I let students discover an idea before I formalize it: before we cover trigonometric equations, for example, students first work through an inquiry-based activity in small groups, so that the method they later learn is one they have already begun to build themselves. Inquiry turns a lesson from mastering a skill into understanding an idea.

I pair this with clear structure, frequent low-stakes practice, and real data and applications, so that students leave my courses with both skill and confidence. Having taught for more than ten years on two continents, I adapt my teaching to students of very different backgrounds and preparation.

Teaching Statistics

Statistics is a central part of my teaching. I have taught Probability and Statistics to engineering and computer science students for several years in Nepal and Introduction to Statistics at UAB, and I hold an M.S. in Applied Mathematics and Statistics (Biostatistics track). In my statistics courses, students work with real data sets, interpret results in context, and use software such as R, Python and Minitab, so that statistical reasoning becomes a practical tool rather than a set of formulas.

I am prepared to teach introductory and applied statistics, probability, statistics for engineers and scientists, regression and data analysis, and Bayesian methods.

Research

Interests: partial differential equations, mathematical biology and chemotaxis, hyperbolic–parabolic systems, computational mathematics, data science, and machine learning.

Dissertation

Global Existence of Solutions on the Quarter Plane for a Chemotaxis Model with Logistic Growth and Logarithmic Sensitivity.

I study a Keller–Segel type system with logarithmic sensitivity and logistic growth on the half-line \(x>0\), of the form

$$ \begin{cases} u_t = D\,u_{xx} - \chi\,\big(u\,(\ln v)_x\big)_x + a\,u\left(1-\dfrac{u}{K}\right),\\[6pt] v_t = \varepsilon\, v_{xx} - \mu\, u\, v, \end{cases} \qquad x>0,\ t>0, $$

subject to initial and boundary data. The goal is to prove that solutions exist globally in time. The main tool is a Nakamura–Nishibata boundary-cancellation energy method, which I extend to the fully nonlinear setting.
